**Match-the-following verification (Section III)**
The printed pairs were checked one by one against the chapter text:
- Bombay with Religious centre: contradicted. The Page 71 print image says "Bombay was initially seven islands". The Page 68 print image describes Bombay as a trading and port centre, and the religious centres named in the chapter are Kedarnath and Badrinath (Page 69 print image).
- Cantonment towns with Hill stations: contradicted. The Page 69 print image introduces cantonment towns as places where "Army people began to live" and gives Kanpur and Lahore as examples. It treats hill stations as a separate category (Simla, Nainital, Darjeeling, Ootacamund and Kodaikanal).
- Kedarnath with Ancient town: contradicted. The Page 69 print image says "Kedarnath and Badrinath were Hindu religious Centres". The ancient towns named on the Page 67 print image are Harappa, Mohenjadaro, Varanasi, Allahabad and Madurai.
- Darjeeling with Seven island: contradicted. The Page 69 print image lists Darjeeling as a hill station that was an alternative capital of Calcutta. The Page 71 print image says "Bombay was initially seven islands".
- Madurai with Kanpur: contradicted. The Page 67 print image lists Madurai among the "well-known ancient towns". The Page 69 print image gives Kanpur as an example of a cantonment town.
- Conclusion: all five printed pairings are contradicted by the chapter's own facts. The table is therefore listed as two unpaired columns, each in printed order. The chapter facts that the table should have reflected are Bombay with Seven island, Cantonment towns with Kanpur, Kedarnath with Religious centre, Darjeeling with Hill stations and Madurai with Ancient town.
